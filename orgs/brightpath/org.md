## The business
Brightpath runs 14 primary-care and urgent-care clinics. About 40% of patients are over 60. Most bookings still come by phone, which ties up two front-desk staff per clinic every morning. Leadership wants more patient tasks to move online.

## Regulators and rules
- HIPAA. Any screen, text, or email that shows a visit reason or provider type is protected health information.
- State rules on minors: a parent can book for a child under 13. Teens 13 to 17 can book some visit types without a parent.
- ADA and WCAG 2.1 AA for every patient-facing screen. Brightpath was sued over its old portal in 2023.

## Who sits where
- Nadia, COO, sponsors operational projects and signs vendor contracts.
- Owen runs IT and owns every system integration.
- Priya is the privacy and compliance officer.
- Marcus is a front-desk lead at the busiest clinic.
- Lena runs patient experience and owns the complaint line.
- Hal is a patient advisory board member, 71, who uses an iPad with large text.

## Culture (assumed)
- Clinicians distrust anything that adds no-shows or double bookings.
- Front-desk staff are the ones who absorb every failure, by phone, the next morning.
- Privacy says no first and negotiates after.

## Decision rights
- Nadia owns the decision to launch or buy.
- Priya can block on privacy. Owen can block on integration risk.
- Lena and Marcus are consulted. Hal is informed and speaks for patients.

## Vocabulary
- "Visit type" means the kind of appointment (annual physical, sick visit, follow-up). Each has a set length.
- "Template" means a provider's weekly grid of bookable slots.
- "Caregiver access" means a person acting for a patient, such as a parent or an adult child.
