## The business (assumed)
Northpine makes Emberfall, a free-to-play co-op action game on PC, PlayStation, Xbox, and Switch. It launched three years ago, peaked at 1.4 million monthly players, and now holds about 600,000. The game makes about $70M a year: battle passes are about 55%, cosmetics in the store about 40%, and a few paid expansions the rest. Players never pay for a gameplay advantage. Northpine treats that as a promise to its community.

Storefronts (Steam, PlayStation, Xbox, Nintendo) take 30% of every sale. Revenue is down 12% year over year. The board wants a second game in the pipeline before Emberfall declines further. Project Lantern, the next game, has 70 people. It is not greenlit yet, and Emberfall's revenue pays for it.

## Regulators and rules
- Platform certification. Sony, Microsoft, and Nintendo each test every patch against their own requirements. A failed submission costs one to two weeks.
- Age ratings from ESRB (United States) and PEGI (Europe). Emberfall is rated T / PEGI 12. Changes to purchases or chat can change the rating.
- Rules on paid random items ("loot boxes"). Belgium bans them, and the Netherlands restricts them. China and South Korea require published drop odds. Emberfall sells no random items today.
- The US Federal Trade Commission (FTC) acts against confusing purchase flows. Its $520M settlement with Epic Games in 2022 is the case everyone cites.
- COPPA (the US children's privacy law) and GDPR. Some players are under 16, so chat and data collection carry age checks.

## Who sits where
- Dana is CEO and co-founder. She sponsors big bets and answers to the board.
- Marcus is game director. He owns Emberfall's creative vision and its roadmap.
- Mira is the live-ops producer. She owns the season calendar and the patch schedule.

- Kofi leads player support and community. He owns tickets, refunds, and player sentiment.
- Ren is the accessibility lead and reviews every new feature.
- Jules is a veteran Emberfall player and content creator with 80,000 followers. Not an employee.
- Sol leads AI tools in the art and dialogue pipeline.

## Culture (assumed)
- Content beats process. A season ships on its date, even if a feature slips to the next one.
- Design and monetization argue in every review. Design says "this feels like a cash grab." Monetization says "this pays for Lantern."
- People remember the 2023 crunch before season 6. Leadership promised "no crunch" in public.
- The community reads patch notes line by line. A bad store change becomes a Reddit thread within an hour.
- Data culture is strong in monetization and weak everywhere else.

## Decision rights (assumed)
- Marcus owns decisions about Emberfall. Dana owns decisions about Lantern and anything with company-level risk.
- Tomas can block on certification or server stability risk.
- Ines can block on rating, consumer law, or privacy risk.
- Helen approves anything that changes the budget by more than $250K.
- Ren can block a new feature that locks out players with disabilities. Leadership has overruled her twice.
- Mira, Priya, and Kofi are consulted. Jules is consulted as the player voice. Sol is informed.

## Vocabulary
- "Season" means a 10-week content cycle with its own battle pass.
- "Cert" means platform certification. "Failing cert" means a platform rejected a build.
- "MAU" means monthly active users. "ARPPU" means average revenue per paying user.
- "Whales" means the top 2% of spenders. Leadership asks people not to use the word in documents.
- "Vertical slice" means a short, polished, playable piece of the new game that the greenlight decision uses.

## Meeting norms (assumed)
- Every proposal states its effect on MAU, revenue, and support tickets.
- Anything players see gets a "how does this read on Reddit" check.
- Decisions go in the review doc, not in Slack.
