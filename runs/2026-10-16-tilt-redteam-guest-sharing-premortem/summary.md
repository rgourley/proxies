## How it failed

Both proxies would block it. Both stories end with the same failure: the weekly AI status summary sent Fernhill's internal comments to its own client. In Sam's story it happened on June 8 and turned Fernhill's renewal into a competitive evaluation. In Ana's it happened on January 11, cost Fernhill a fee, and nearly cost it a client.

## Failure stories that both proxies told

| # | What failed | Severity | Told by |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The weekly AI summary was written from internal comments and sent to guests. A comment about billing extra rounds, or about the client delaying the timeline, reached the client | Critical | Sam, Ana |
| 2 | Nobody at Fernhill could see every outside person in one place. By mid-June there were about 260 guests across 31 projects, and no one knew the number | Critical | Sam, Ana |

## What to fix first

1. **Write the guest summary only from what a guest can see**, or leave guests off the summary. (Sam, Ana)
2. **A workspace-wide list of guests**, with who invited each one and what they can see. (Sam, Ana)
3. **Tie each magic link to the invited email, and end every session when a guest is removed.** (Sam)
