# Rosa, Freelance illustrator

**Verdict**: Block. A required phone number on the first screen is where I close the tab, and I never send an invoice.

## 1. Required phone number on the sign-up form

- **Where**: The create-your-account screen.
- **How it breaks**: I'm on my phone between two client calls. I've filled in eight fields with my thumbs. Then it wants my phone number, and I can't skip it. I haven't even seen the product yet, so I'm not handing over my number. I close the tab. I tell myself I'll try again tonight, and I won't. My client still doesn't have an invoice.
- **Severity**: Critical. This breaks one of my hard lines, so for me the flow just stops here.
- **Fix**: Remove the phone number field. If you ever need it, ask for it later, after I've sent an invoice, and let me skip it.

## 2. Nine fields on a phone, and the labels disappear while I type

- **Where**: The create-your-account screen.
- **How it breaks**: The labels are grey text inside each box, and they vanish once I start typing. On a small screen I scroll, the keyboard covers half the form, and I lose track of which box is "Company name" and which is "Confirm password." I'm a freelancer. I don't have a company size or a "work email." Then it asks how I heard about you, and that's required too. Ten minutes is gone before I've seen an invoice.
- **Severity**: High.
- **Fix**: Ask only for email and password. Put labels above the fields so they stay visible. Make company name optional, and move company size and "how did you hear" out of sign-up or drop them.

## 3. The error clears my passwords and doesn't say what's wrong

- **Where**: What happens when the form has errors.
- **How it breaks**: I type a ten-character password, because nobody told me the rules before I hit Submit. A red banner says "There was a problem with your submission." Both password boxes are now empty. Some boxes have red borders, with no reason given. I skim, I miss the rules line, I retype the same password on my phone keyboard, and it fails again. That's the second time I bail.
- **Severity**: High.
- **Fix**: Show the password rules under the field before I type. Keep everything I entered after an error. Put a plain message next to each field that's wrong, such as "Needs a symbol."

## 4. Check-your-email is a dead end, and the link dies in 15 minutes

- **Where**: The check-your-email screen.
- **How it breaks**: My phone autocorrects my email to "rosa@gmial.com." The screen just says "We sent a link to your email." It doesn't show the address, so I can't see the typo, change it, or ask for a resend. Or the email does arrive, but my next call starts and I open it 20 minutes later, after the link has expired. Either way I'm stuck, and there's no button on this screen that helps me.
- **Severity**: High.
- **Fix**: Show the address on the screen, with "Wrong email? Change it" and a resend button. Make the link last at least 24 hours.

## 5. A forced team survey, then an empty dashboard with no "New invoice" button

- **Where**: The tell-us-about-your-team screen and the dashboard.
- **How it breaks**: I'm one person, and it asks about my "team" with no way to skip. It asks for team size a second time. It wants me to type out my "biggest invoicing challenge" before I can continue. My answer is "this sign-up." When I finally get in, I see an empty table. It doesn't tell me what to do, and nothing on the screen says "New invoice." It's hidden in the menu at the top right, and I wouldn't think to look there.
- **Severity**: Medium. By now I'm almost in, but I might still give up before I find the button.
- **Fix**: Make the team screen skippable, or cut it. When the table is empty, put a big "Create your first invoice" button in the middle of it.

Smaller things I noticed. The Google, Microsoft, and Apple sign-in buttons are icons only and sit under the long form. They're the fastest way in on a phone, and I'd scroll right past them. Put them at the top, with words on them. Also, the box I have to tick bundles the terms with marketing emails, so I can't agree to the terms without also signing up for marketing. Split those into two separate boxes.
