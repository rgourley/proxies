# Maya, New retail trader, eight months in stocks, no options yet

## Build

**Leon's paper month with the ugly parts, joined to Ruth's day-by-day replay.** Leon is right that a clean simulator would teach me the wrong thing. Ruth is right that I learn by watching my own number shrink. So put them together. When I get assigned on paper, the replay should stop on that day. It should show one line: "You now own 100 shares at $55. That is $5,500. Your real account has $2,100." Show the pretend loss next to my real balance. I keep thinking a small account means small risk. I need the app to put my real balance next to the bill, so I can see whether I could pay it. I'd also ask Leon one thing. Can I see the ugly events listed after each paper week? I won't know a gap overnight happened unless someone tells me.

**Ruth's weekly "check yourself" question.** I'd answer this, but only if it reaches me on my phone in the evening, when I'm actually trading. One question, two or three answer buttons, done in under a minute. If I get it wrong, show the worked example right away using my own trade. Don't send me to an article. Also, never show a score. If I see "2 out of 5," I'll stop answering.

## Challenge

**Sol's made-up tickers and made-up prices.** I get the legal reason. But if the lesson uses a fake company called "ABC Corp," I'll skip it. I care about the stocks I already own. A fake example feels like homework, and the confident video about a real stock will win my attention every time. That's the risk. The lesson loses to the video, and I learn from the video instead. What would answer it: let the practice account use real prices on stocks I already hold, while the lesson itself never has a "trade it" button. Can Sol accept that split?

## New idea

**A "what did I just do?" button on every open position.** Ruth said her students panic and call for help. That will be me. One tap gives three plain lines: what I own, the most I can still lose, and what it costs to close it right now. No chat queue, no guide. Just the answer at 9pm, before I do something worse.
