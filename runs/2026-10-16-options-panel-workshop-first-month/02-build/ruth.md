# Ruth, Financial educator, runs a free investing course for adults

## Build

**Leon's paper month that includes the ugly parts.** Leon is right that a clean simulator teaches false confidence. What I would add is a short debrief right after each ugly event, using the learner's own numbers. Here is an example. "You sold a put at $45 on a $48 stock. The stock opened at $41 after earnings. You were assigned 100 shares at $45, which cost $4,500. On paper, those shares are now worth $4,100. You are down $400 before the premium you collected." Then, a week later, the simulator brings back a similar setup on purpose. The first assignment is a shock. The second one is where the learner actually learns.

**Sol's knowledge check before the next approval level, and Leon's test before selling.** These are really one gate, so build it once. Every question should be a worked example with real dollar amounts, not a definition. Don't ask "What is assignment?" Ask "You sold one put at $30 and the stock closes at $27. What do you owe?" When a learner gets one wrong, show them the worked answer and let them retake the check without waiting and without a mark on their record. The firm records that they passed. It does not need to tell them how many tries it took. If adults feel judged, they stop trying.

## Challenge

**Sol's lessons that use only made-up tickers.** I understand the regulatory reason. My worry is transfer. In my classes, a lesson about "Stock XYZ at $50" teaches the idea. It does not stick until learners see it on a position they chose themselves. Here is what would answer this. Lessons use made-up tickers. The practice debrief can use whatever practice trade the learner picked on their own, with no button that leads to a real order. Sol, I need you to tell us whether a debrief on a trade the customer chose counts as a recommendation.

## New idea

**A day-90 return visit.** At three months, the app re-asks the one knowledge-check question each learner got wrong first. It asks with fresh numbers. The month-one results tell us who passed. They don't tell us who still understands assignment in month three.
