# Sol, Compliance officer at a retail brokerage

## Build

**Leon's "start with buying only, and unlock selling by passing a test."** Leon's version is stricter than mine. Month one allows buying calls and puts only. I accept that. Here is what I would add so it holds up in an exam. The test answers go into the customer's account file, with a date and the version of the test they took. The upgrade to selling is a firm approval decision under Financial Industry Regulatory Authority (FINRA) Rule 2360, so a registered principal signs off on it. A passed quiz alone does not upgrade anyone. Questions come from a rotating pool, and a wrong answer shows the right answer with a worked example. That way nobody passes by memorising the answer order.

**Ruth's "what happens Friday" card on every open position.** This is the cleanest idea in the room from my seat. It describes the customer's own position. It does not suggest a new trade. I would add three things. First, the card shows facts and no actions. "If the stock closes at $56, you will be assigned" passes. "Consider closing before Friday" is a recommendation. Second, Ruth is right that a wrong number is worse than no card. If the app cannot compute the outcome with confidence, the card says so and shows nothing. Third, we keep a record of each version of the card the customer saw, as Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) Rule 17a-4 requires for customer communications.

## Challenge

**Maya's "what happens if" card before every trade.** I like the dollar amounts. The risk is in the scenarios we pick. "If it drops 5%, you lose all $240" makes a 5% drop sound like the likely case. FINRA Rule 2210 forbids projections of performance in communications. FINRA Rule 2214 allows an investment analysis tool only with clear disclosures about its limits. What answers it: fixed, mechanical scenarios on every trade, the same for every customer. Those are "stock does not move," "stock ends at the strike price," and "option expires worthless." The maximum loss always appears first. The card shows no probabilities and no upside-only scenario. One plain line says these are examples, not forecasts.

## New idea

**A month-one record the customer can read.** Maya said she thinks $200 is low risk and is not sure. At the end of month one, send each customer a one-page summary. It lists the risk notices they saw, the test results, and how their practice trades ended. The customer uses it to check themselves. We use the same page as the evidence file for any approval upgrade.
