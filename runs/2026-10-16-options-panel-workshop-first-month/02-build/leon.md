# Leon, Full-time options trader, 11 years

## Build

**Ruth's "run every first trade twice" replay.** I'd keep it. But Ruth's example is a quiet stock and slow decay. Decay is the gentle lesson. The replay also needs a bad version. Run the same trade a second time through a bad week. The stock gaps down 15% overnight on earnings. The fill comes in at the worse side of the bid-ask spread, which is the gap between what buyers pay and what sellers accept. If the trade sold an option, the learner gets assigned early, on Wednesday, not Friday. Then show both endings side by side, the quiet week and the bad week. My first loss came from the bad week. Nobody had ever shown me that version.

**Ruth's "what happens Friday" card plus Maya's expiry alerts.** These should be one thing. The alert two days out should open Ruth's card for that exact position, with Maya's plain wording. I'd add one line most apps leave out. The stock can keep trading after the 4pm close on Friday. So "you're safe, it's below the strike" can be wrong by Saturday morning. The strike is the agreed price in the contract. The card should say: "If you are close to the agreed price, close the trade before 3pm Friday."

## Challenge

**Sol's first-month approval level.** It lets people sell cash-secured puts in month one. That's a put you sell while you hold enough cash to buy the shares. That is exactly the trade that got me. I sold a put, the stock gapped down 18%, and I got 300 shares at a price far above market. "Cash-secured" sounds safe. It only means you can afford the loss. It doesn't mean the loss is small. The answer: no selling of any kind in month one, or a new trader must take an assignment on paper before they can sell.

## New idea

**A loss breakdown after every losing trade.** When a trade loses money, real or paper, the app shows one screen that splits the loss into parts. It shows how much came from the stock moving, how much from time passing, and how much from the bid-ask spread. Most beginners blame the stock. Half the time it was the clock or the spread. Show them which, in dollars.
