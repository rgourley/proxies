## The answer the room converged on

Month one runs on practice money that fails the way real money fails. Real trades are limited to buying calls and puts. Every position shows, in dollars, what happens on Friday. Moving up to selling options takes a test made of worked examples, and the result is kept on file.

Nobody argued for letting beginners sell options in month one. Leon, who lost money that way on his own first trade, argued against it hardest.

## Themes

### 1. Practice that hurts like the real thing
- **A paper month with the ugly parts** (Leon): early assignment, overnight gaps, and fills at the worse side of the bid-ask spread, the gap between the buying and selling price. Built on by Maya, who wants the replay to stop on the day of assignment and show the cash she would have needed, and by Ruth, who wants a short debrief after each bad event, using the trader's own numbers.
- **Run every first trade twice** (Ruth): the same trade on paper, replayed day by day to expiry. Built on by Leon, who wants a second replay through a bad week, not only a quiet one.
- **A practice month that feels real** (Maya): proposed in round 1, then merged into Leon's idea by Maya herself.

### 2. Show the dollars before and during the trade
- **The "what happens Friday" card on every open position** (Ruth). Built on by Leon, who wants Maya's expiry alerts to open this card, with a warning that the stock can keep moving after Friday's close. Built on by Sol, who calls it "the cleanest idea in the room" because it describes the customer's own position and suggests no trade. Sol's condition: the card states facts and no actions.
- **The worst-case screen before every order** (Leon) and **the "what happens if" card** (Maya): the most you can lose, what happens at expiry, and the cash you would need if assigned. Challenged by Sol (see open questions).
- **Expiry warnings in plain words** (Maya): merged into the Friday card by Leon.

### 3. Earn the next step by understanding, not by trading more
- **Buying only in month one, selling unlocked by a test** (Leon) and **a first-month approval level** (Sol). Ruth merged these into one gate built from worked examples with real dollar amounts, not definitions. Sol accepted Leon's stricter version and added that the test answers go on file as evidence for the approval.
- **The weekly "check yourself" question** (Ruth). Built on by Maya: one question on her phone, answered in under a minute, a worked example right away when she gets it wrong, and never a score.

### 4. Records the customer and the firm can both use
- **Risk notices inside the order ticket, saved with a timestamp** (Sol).
- **A month-one record the customer can read** (Sol, new in round 2): the notices seen, the test results, and how the practice trades ended. It doubles as the evidence file for an upgrade.

## Open questions

- **Which scenarios does the pre-trade card show?** Sol warned that "if it drops 5%, you lose all $240" makes a 5% drop sound likely. That can count as a projection of performance under the Financial Industry Regulatory Authority's (FINRA) communication rules. Sol's answer is fixed, mechanical scenarios with a stated limit. Nobody tested whether Maya would still read a card like that.
- **Real tickers or made-up ones?** Sol wants lessons on made-up tickers, so that education never reads as a recommendation. Maya and Ruth both challenged this: Maya skips "ABC Corp" lessons, and Ruth says learning sticks only on a position the learner chose. Ruth's middle path: lessons use made-up tickers, and the practice debrief uses whatever trade the learner picked on their own. Sol has not answered it.
- **Should month one allow cash-secured puts?** Sol's approval level allowed them. Leon challenged this from his own loss. The room leaned toward Leon's buying-only version, and Sol accepted it in round 2.

## Worth keeping

**The loss breakdown after every losing trade** (Leon, new in round 2): one screen that splits a loss into how much came from the stock moving, how much from time passing, and how much from the spread. Nobody built on it, because it arrived in the last round. But it answers the misunderstanding that every other idea circles: beginners blame the stock, and, as Leon put it, "half the time it was the clock or the spread". Ruth's day-90 return visit, also new in round 2, would check whether that lesson stuck.
