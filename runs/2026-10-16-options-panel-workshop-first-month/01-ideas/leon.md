# Leon, Full-time options trader, 11 years

My first real options trade was a short put on a stock I liked. I sold it on a Thursday and it gapped down 18% on earnings the next week. I got assigned 300 shares I could barely pay for. I had no idea assignment could happen like that. Every idea below aims at that exact moment.

## 1. A paper-trading month before real money

**Idea:** The first 30 days run on paper only, which means simulated trades with fake money. The simulation has to include the ugly parts: early assignment, expiry, wide bid-ask spreads (the gap between the buying price and the selling price), and price gaps overnight.

**Why it matters to me:** Most paper trading fills you at the perfect price and never assigns you. That teaches false confidence. I want a beginner to get assigned on paper and feel it before it costs them rent.

**What it assumes:** The broker will build a simulator that loses money realistically, even though a "clean" simulator makes beginners feel better.

## 2. A worst-case screen before every order

**Idea:** Before a new trader places an order, one screen shows three things in plain words. It shows the most they can lose. It shows what happens if they hold the position through expiry. It shows what happens if they get assigned, including the cash they would need.

**Why it matters to me:** Beginners lose money because nobody shows them the downside in dollars. "Undefined risk" means nothing to a new trader. "You could owe $14,000" does.

**What it assumes:** The broker will show the scary number even when it lowers how many orders people place.

## 3. Start with buying only, and unlock selling by passing a test

**Idea:** Month one allows only buying calls and puts, where the most you can lose is what you paid. Selling options comes later, after the trader passes a short test on assignment and margin calls (a demand for more cash when the account falls too low).

**Why it matters to me:** This is how I would teach my own mentees. And no badges or streak rewards. You earn the next step by understanding risk, not by trading more often.

**What it assumes:** The test checks real understanding, not clicking through.
