# Maya, New retail trader, eight months in stocks, no options yet

I've watched maybe fifty options videos. I still couldn't tell you what happens on Friday if I'm holding a contract and do nothing. So here is what I'd actually use.

## 1. The "what happens if" card before every trade

**Idea**: Before I tap buy, the app shows one small card in plain words. "If the stock doesn't move by Friday, you lose $180. If it drops 5%, you lose all $240. You cannot lose more than $240." No charts and no Greek letters, just dollar amounts for a few scenarios.

**Why it matters to me**: I trade at 9pm on my phone. I won't open a guide, but I will read four lines that name the money I could lose. I don't need theory. I need to see the dollar number before I tap.

**What it assumes**: The app can turn each trade into honest dollar outcomes that a beginner reads correctly the first time.

## 2. A practice month with fake money that feels real

**Idea**: My first month runs on a paper account that copies real prices, plus some real trades capped at a small amount. Paper means pretend money. The app tells me when a pretend trade would have gone badly, and why.

**Why it matters to me**: I'd rather get wrecked with pretend money first. I think $200 is low risk because my account is small. Honestly, I'm not sure that's right, and I want to find out without paying for it.

**What it assumes**: People take the practice account seriously. It can't feel like a game.

## 3. Warnings when expiry is close, in plain words

**Idea**: Push alerts two days and one day before something expires. They say what will happen: "This expires Friday. If you do nothing, it becomes worthless," or "you may have to buy 100 shares."

**Why it matters to me**: Nobody in the videos explains assignment, the rule that can force me to buy or sell shares. That's the part that scares me. I want the app to warn me before it happens, not explain afterward.

**What it assumes**: The alerts are rare enough that I don't mute them.
