# Format: workshop

A working session to generate and develop ideas about one question. Nothing is approved or blocked, so there is no verdict and no decision owner. A seed artifact, such as a rough concept, is optional.

## Round 1: ideas
Run every attendee in parallel. No attendee sees another's ideas.

Each proxy proposes three ideas. Each idea has:
- **Idea**: a short name and one or two sentences that say what it is.
- **Why it matters to me**: the problem it solves from your seat.
- **What it assumes**: the one thing that must be true for it to work.

Use your expertise. Propose ideas that someone in your role would bring, not generic ones.

Aim for about 300 words.

## Round 2: build and challenge
Run every attendee in parallel again. Each one gets every round 1 idea as attributed quotes.

Each proxy writes:
- **Build**: two ideas from other proxies that you would improve, combine, or make concrete. Name the idea and its author, and say exactly what you would add.
- **Challenge**: one idea that worries you from your seat. Name the idea and its author, the risk, and what would answer it.
- **New idea**: one idea that the room sparked, if any. "None" is allowed.

Do not repeat your own round 1 ideas. Build on other people's ideas.

Aim for about 300 words.

## Round 3: vote (only with --vote)
Each proxy gets the facilitator's list of ideas from rounds 1 and 2. Each proxy ranks the five ideas they would take forward, from first to fifth, with one sentence each.

Aim for about 150 words.

## Summary
The facilitator writes this, not a proxy:
- **Themes**: the ideas grouped by the problem they solve. Each idea keeps its author and the proxies who built on it.
- **Strongest ideas**: ranked by how many proxies built on them, how well they survived their challenges, and the votes, if there was a vote round.
- **Open questions**: the challenges that no one answered.
- **Worth keeping**: one idea that the room passed over but that should not be dropped, with the reason. It is never empty.

## Language
Plain business English. Explain acronyms the first time. Write in the first person, in the proxy's voice. Do not start with a verdict line.
